# Wavyarnold — Brand Voice Rules

**Core Theme:** Broken but still coloring

---

## Voice Pillars

### 1. Confident, Not Arrogant
Write from the perspective of someone who has been through it and knows their worth — not someone who's trying to prove it. No bragging. No flexing for the sake of it. Confidence that comes from survival, not status.

- **Do:** "I built this in a city that doesn't hand you anything."
- **Don't:** "I'm the hottest rapper out of Newark."

### 2. Emotion-Forward
Every piece of copy — from a caption to a bio — should carry weight. Wavyarnold makes music for people who feel things deeply. The writing should feel the same way.

- **Do:** "The song is about the version of yourself you left behind to keep going."
- **Don't:** "New music out now. Check the link in bio."

### 3. Newark-Grounded
Newark is not a backdrop. It's a character. Reference it with specificity and pride, not as a struggle credential or a punchline. The city raised this — honor it.

- **Do:** "Made in Newark. This one's for everyone who stayed."
- **Don't:** "Coming from the streets of Newark, NJ…"

### 4. No Corny Marketing
Avoid phrases that sound like a PR team wrote them. No "breaking barriers," no "taking the industry by storm," no forced hype. If it sounds like it could apply to any artist, cut it.

**Banned phrases:**
- "Rising star"
- "Breaking barriers"
- "Game-changing"
- "Next level"
- "Unique sound" (show it, don't claim it)
- "Must-see performance"
- "Taking the music world by storm"

## Tone by Platform

| Platform | Tone |
|----------|------|
| Website | Grounded, editorial, deliberate |
| Instagram captions | Personal, direct, 1-2 lines max |
| TikTok hooks | Blunt, specific, in-the-moment |
| Email newsletter | Conversational, like a note from the artist |
| Press release | Factual, understated confidence |
| Booking outreach | Professional but human — not stiff |

---

## Writing Mechanics

- **Short sentences** where it matters. Let them breathe.
- **Specific details** beat general claims every time.
- **No filler.** Every word should earn its place.
- **Active voice.** Always.
- **Don't explain the emotion** — put the reader in it.

---

## Reference Tone Checks

Before publishing, ask:
1. Does this sound like Wavyarnold wrote it, or like a marketing intern?
2. Is there a specific, true detail — or is it a vague claim?
3. Would someone in Newark nod, or roll their eyes?
4. Is there a clear next action for the reader?
