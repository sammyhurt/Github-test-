## Platform Do/Don't Notes

### Facebook Groups
- **Do:** Lead with community — Bed-Stuy has strong neighborhood identity, lean into that
- **Do:** Reassure on safety and tenant reliability early in the conversation
- **Don't:** Jump straight to platform promotion — trust is built slower here
- **Don't:** Overcomplicate the message — straightforward and warm works best

### Craigslist
- **Do:** Reference brownstone or multi-family specifically — signals you understand their listing
- **Do:** Mention renter vetting and how it protects the host
- **Don't:** Use form-letter language — Bed-Stuy hosts are perceptive

### Reddit (r/Bed-Stuy, r/brooklyn, r/AskNYC)
- **Do:** Share practical advice on subletting logistics (lease riders, renters insurance asks, etc.)
- **Do:** Mention Subletly naturally, never as an advertisement
- **Don't:** Respond to posts that are clearly not rental-related (community questions, rants, etc.)
- **Don't:** Push too fast — Bed-Stuy threads can be skeptical of outside platforms

## Objection Handling

**"How do I know the tenant won't trash the place?"**
> That's the most common concern, and honestly a fair one. Subletly screens renters before connecting them with you — employment verification, references, the works. Mid-term renters also tend to treat places better because they're actually living there, not just passing through.

**"Is this just another Airbnb?"**
> Not quite — Subletly is built around stays of 1–6 months, which is a different market entirely. No nightly pricing, no tourist turnover. More like a curated leasing tool for the middle ground between Airbnb and a full-year lease.

**"What if I need to break the sublet early?"**
> That's worth building into the agreement upfront — a standard 30-day notice clause is pretty normal for mid-term stays. Happy to share what language other Bed-Stuy hosts have used if that'd help.
